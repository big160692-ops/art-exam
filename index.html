🎨 Importance of Art

Art is a diverse range of human activities and the products of those activities, central to human life and culture.

Personal Expression

Satisfies feelings, unleashes passion, and allows individuals to express their inner world.

Perspective

Challenges us to see the world differently and consider new viewpoints.

Truth

Reveals insights we may not have understood before, uncovering hidden realities.

Pleasure

Gives satisfaction and gratification to both the creator and the audience.

🖌️ Elements of Art

The building blocks and tools artists use to create art.

Color

Striking visual element that sets the mood and tone of an artwork.

Value

The lightness or darkness of a color; creates depth and contrast.

Texture

The tactile or visual surface quality of an artwork.

Space

The positive and negative voids within a composition.

Line

Vertical, horizontal, jagged, zig-zag; defines shapes and creates movement.

Shape & Form

Organic or geometric shapes; 2D (shape) vs 3D (form).

🖼️ Art Movements

Fauvism
"Wild Beasts" - Bright, bold colors and fierce brushwork. Rejected realistic color in favor of emotional expression.
Dadaism
Embraced absurdity and irrationality. "Hobby-horse" reflects its playful, anti-art nature. A reaction to WWI.
Futurism
Celebrated speed, technology, and modernity. Founded on Marinetti's Manifesto.
Surrealism
"Super-realism" - Making the ordinary extraordinary. Explored dreams and the unconscious.
Impressionism
Focused on optical realism, light, and movement. Captured fleeting moments through visible brushstrokes.

🔹 Forms of Abstraction

Distortion

Altering proportions of forms to create expressive or unsettling effects.

Elongation

Stretching forms beyond natural proportions for stylistic emphasis.

Mangling

Mutilated, torn, or hacked forms - raw and fragmented expression.

Symbolism

Objects representing abstract ideas beyond their literal meaning.

Cubism

Pioneered by Braque & Picasso. Uses geometric shapes and multiple viewpoints simultaneously.

Abstract Expressionism

Post-WWII movement centered in New York. Emotional force and spontaneous, large-scale works.
